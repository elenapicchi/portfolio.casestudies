Rebuilding Ruby Cup's website into a mobile-first shopping experience

A full rebrand meant the old site no longer matched who Ruby Cup had become. I briefed and managed a cross-functional team of 10 to take it from a dated, desktop-first layout to a mobile-first experience built around how customers actually shop.

The brief

A brand that had outgrown its site

Ruby Cup's rebrand nailed down a sharper mission, position and visual identity — but the website was still telling the old story. My job was to translate the new brand into a site that felt as good to shop as the products felt to use, without losing the heritage and social-impact story that made Ruby Cup, Ruby Cup.

A before-and-after scroll through the redesigned site

What changed

  • Moved to a fully mobile-first layout, designed around how most traffic actually arrived — on a phone, not a desktop
  • Redesigned the page flow end-to-end, rethinking the order and structure of information as customers scrolled
  • Built a moodboard and directed new product photography — using two UGC creators instead of a traditional photoshoot, a far more cost-effective approach — then personally edited and designed the final imagery for every product
  • Streamlined product information so key details (fit, materials, impact) were scannable, not buried
  • Gave the site more movement, animation and colour, matching the energy of the new brand identity
  • Briefed and directed a copywriter, UX/UI expert, designer, and ads specialists, and liaised directly with the external web agency

Building bundles from scratch

Product bundles didn't exist on the site before this project. I built them from the ground up — naming, copy, concept and product photography — then added them directly to the product page as a new purchase option.

Making the product page work harder

Beyond the bundles, I added a belt of real UGC imagery directly on the product page, so customers could see the product in actual use before buying — not just studio shots. I also built out interactive step-by-step "how to use" guidance on the page itself, addressing hesitation at the exact moment it happens rather than burying it in an FAQ page customers might never find.

Result

The redesign drove a +92% improvement in conversion rate, and the new bundles lifted average order value by 2%. The project also served a wider goal — making the UK Ruby Cup's primary sales market — and over this period UK sales grew 108%, making it the brand's second most profitable market.
