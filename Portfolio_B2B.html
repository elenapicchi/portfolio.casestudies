The role

From marketing execution to creating the opportunities

When I joined Ruby Cup as Marketing Manager in October 2023, the B2B side of the business was effectively non-existent. There were no sales collaterals, no B2B content strategy and no coordinated external agency infrastructure. The challenge was to build the machinery that could tell Ruby Cup's story to the right corporate audiences.

I worked across the full journey: creating and writing website content, developing LinkedIn communications and paid activity, organising and attending events, sourcing partnership opportunities, representing the brand in relevant rooms, and building relationships with organisations that could create either commercial or strategic value.

Website

Writing the B2B proposition from the ground up

I created and wrote the B2B website content that gave potential partners a clear reason to work with Ruby Cup. The work covered partnership messaging, corporate social responsibility, workplace wellbeing, sustainability and social impact.

Ruby Cup · Partner With Us — open page

Ruby Cup · CSR programme — open page

The CSR proposition was particularly important: rather than presenting Ruby Cup simply as a product purchase, the messaging connected employee support with environmental benefits and the brand's buy-one-give-one social mission. The live CSR page explains the programme through employee support, matched donations and the reduction of waste associated with reusable period products. 

  • Wrote and structured the B2B website copy for corporate audiences.
  • Translated Ruby Cup's social mission into commercially relevant workplace wellbeing, CSR and sustainability messaging.
  • Developed content that could support partnership conversations, rather than functioning as purely informational website copy.
  • Worked within the wider website redevelopment alongside UX/UI, design, development and advertising specialists.

The wider redevelopment was managed across a 10-person cross-functional team and ultimately contributed to a reported 92% improvement in conversion rate through cleaner user journeys, stronger on-page copy and A/B testing. 

Events · Owned

Creating events from the first idea to the last guest

I also hosted and produced events for Ruby Cup — not just turning up on the day, but managing the entire experience behind them.

  • Finding and assessing the right venue for the audience and concept.
  • Developing the event concept and shaping the reason people should attend.
  • Hiring and briefing promoters and other external suppliers.
  • Building and finalising the guest list and managing invitations.
  • Creating goody bags and sourcing partner contributions.
  • Coordinating music, food, suppliers and the on-the-night experience.
  • Managing the event itself and representing Ruby Cup throughout.

One example was Ruby Cup's Menstrual Health Month activity, bringing together guests and partners around women's health, menstrual health and period equity. The resulting LinkedIn post highlights the room full of guests and partners from organisations including Bupa, Human Practice Foundation, Danish-UK Association and Eeko Media, alongside NGO and health-sector partners. 

Events · Representation

Showing up where the right B2B conversations happen

Not every opportunity starts with a direct inbound lead. I actively looked for events and communities where Ruby Cup could meet relevant corporate, sustainability, wellbeing and partnership audiences, and represented the brand in those spaces with the aim of developing B2B relationships.

That meant identifying useful rooms, attending on behalf of the brand, starting conversations, explaining the proposition and following up on potential opportunities rather than treating events as passive brand exposure.

What this work demonstrates

A partnership-led approach to B2B marketing

Across Ruby Cup and Vonder, the common thread was finding a useful intersection between a brand and another organisation's audience, product or expertise — then turning that intersection into something tangible.

Opportunity sourcing Finding relevant events, communities, podcasts, brands and organisations before they became obvious inbound opportunities.
Partnership development Starting conversations, shaping the value exchange, negotiating terms and moving collaborations towards execution.
Brand representation Showing up in the right rooms and turning conversations into potential commercial and strategic relationships.
B2B copywriting Making a social-impact proposition clear, credible and relevant to corporate decision-makers.
Event production Managing the experience end to end, from concept and venue to guests, suppliers, content and follow-up.
Cross-functional management Coordinating external agencies, specialists and partners alongside the day-to-day marketing programme.

Results

Building the infrastructure for the brand to keep showing up

The result was a broader B2B marketing engine: clearer website propositions, consistent professional content, paid and organic LinkedIn activity, owned events, external event representation, partnership opportunities and a network of organisations that could extend Ruby Cup's reach.

The wider website redevelopment improved conversion by 92%, while the B2B programme moved Ruby Cup from having effectively no enterprise infrastructure to securing its first corporate clients.

“The challenge was building the machinery to tell it.”

That is ultimately what this work was about: building the machinery while creating the opportunities for a mission-led brand to be visible, credible and commercially relevant.
